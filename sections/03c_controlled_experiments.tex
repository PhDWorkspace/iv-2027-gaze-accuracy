
\subsection{Controlled Experiments}
\label{sec:method_controlled}

To investigate the factors underlying gaze variability
observed during driving, we designed a complementary
controlled study that systematically varies environmental,
geometric, and dynamic conditions. The experiments address
challenges encountered in real driving, including distant
targets, changing illumination, peripheral gaze, and
tracking moving objects.

The current dataset comprises 216 recordings (2.9\,h)
from six participants, including repeated measurements
from participants of the driving study. Static fixation
experiments use the 15 inner corners of chessboard
patterns presented on screens or printed targets.
QR-coded protocol identification enables automated
recognition of the experimental configuration and
target references, followed by manual verification.
A separate smooth-pursuit protocol evaluates dynamic
gaze tracking along a predefined target trajectory.

Repeated recordings and forward/reverse fixation
sequences support the investigation of temporal and
spatial effects. For each fixation, we evaluate the
central 60\% of the interval to reduce contamination
from gaze transitions. The resulting dataset provides
systematically characterized reference measurements
for distinguishing systematic gaze errors from
measurement dispersion.
