# Related Work: Gaze Supervision and Driving Datasets

**Created:** 2026-11-02
**Purpose:** Source material for the *Gaze Supervision and Driving Datasets* subsection of the IEEE IV 2027 paper. Papers are ranked by how directly they bear on our contribution as defined in `phd-literature/skills/contribution_gaze_supervision_driving_datasets.md` (C1 real-driving target-referenced benchmark; C2 measuring and correcting the gaze signal; C3 quality-aware acquisition).
**Source pool:** 136 summaries in `phd-literature/summaries/`, the PDFs in `phd-literature/papers/`, and one external paper (Jakobi et al.), which was read from arXiv because it is not in the library.
**BibTeX keys:** every key below was checked against `phd-literature/bib/references.bib`. The one exception is marked **[NOT IN BIB]**.

## How to read this file

- **Verification status**
  - **[V-PDF]**: the statement was checked against the full text of the PDF. `p.N` is the **PDF page index**, which may differ from the printed journal page.
  - **[V-arXiv]**: checked against the arXiv full text (used only for `jakobi2026livingSurvey`).
  - **[S]**: from our summary only and not re-checked in the PDF. Check the PDF before citing an [S] statement.
- **"Not reported" / "not found"** means we did not find it in the PDF. It does **not** mean the authors never did it.
- **Scope of evidence:** each statement concerns only the cited paper. Our own numbers come from the contribution file and appear only in the "Gap / relevance for us" parts.
- **Terminology:** following the contribution file, "accuracy" here means **angular gaze-coordinate error against an identified target reference**. Saliency metrics (KLD, CC, NSS, SIM), detection AP and policy performance are **not** accuracy in this sense.

## 3. Limitations of current datasets (evidence catalogue)

Each row is one limitation. **Our relevance** says which part of our work (C1/C2/C3 in the contribution file) addresses it, and how far. We must not overclaim.

| # | Limitation | Evidence (key, page) | Our relevance (and its limits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **The accuracy of the gaze signal is not reported against identified targets.** Driving-gaze datasets report calibration procedures or device specifications, not measured angular error during the recording. | DR(eye)VE measured ETG error on 20 observers at 5–15 m only to set a Gaussian width; no value is reported (`palazzi2019dreyeve` p.4). MAAD quotes the device spec of 0.15–0.50° (`gopinath2021maad` p.6). LAIA *assumes* <5 px device error (`contreras2026laia` p.7). Stapel reports a calibration-time typical 1.2° for a remote tracker (`stapel2022driverPerception` p.6). Gong uses a one-point calibration with no accuracy value (`gong2026eegDataset` p.3). No accuracy measurement was found in LBW, DADA, DriverGaze360 or Zhao et al. | **C1/C2.** Angular error, bias, dispersion and precision against verified targets in real driving. Limits: 2 participants; announced fixations; held-out scenes, not drives or days. |
| L2 | **The eye-tracker → scene mapping is noisy and unvalidated.** | Uncalibrated homography: median error within 20 px for most DR(eye)VE videos, but >13% of frames are outliers and half of those exceed 200 px, especially at night or in rain; error grows with horizontal eccentricity (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.4). The 1-s temporal re-projection adds error: 12–14% of outliers exceed 100 px, most >200 px at night (p.5). LBW uses the same approach; many frames are wrong under large head rotations, but the errors "could not be estimated" (p.4–5). Failure cases (looking down, mirror, outside the roof view, raindrops) are shown in `kotseruba2024taskContext` p.9–10. LBW relies on a far-scene assumption (`kasahara2022lookBothWays` p.6). Zhao et al. use a fixed pixel scaling (`zhao2023gazeVision` p.4). | **C1, partly.** We evaluate in the **eye-tracker scene image** (no cross-camera projection in the accuracy metric). Our use of vehicle cameras/LiDAR for target range is a separate step; we should state whether its alignment was validated. |
| L3 | **Temporal synchronization errors and missing timestamps.** | DR(eye)VE videos "did not contain timestamps"; most were misaligned by 7 ± 5 frames, 40% by up to 15 frames (0.5 s), 10% by >25 frames (1 s); re-alignment changed ≈90% of ETG–GAR correspondences (`kotseruba2024taskContext` p.3). DR(eye)VE vehicle data lags "in one instance, by nearly a minute" (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.3). LBW is missing 26.5% of frames, unevenly (32–35% during lateral manoeuvres) (p.3). BDD-A clips contain repeated or dropped frames (p.3). Gong et al. describe a sync scheme but report no measured alignment error (`gong2026eegDataset` p.4). | **C1, partly.** PTP/GNSS-disciplined clock + Neon clock-offset estimation. We should report the measured residual offset, if we have it, rather than only describe the scheme. |
| L4 | **Non-fixation samples and out-of-view gaze are mixed into the labels.** | Blinks and saccades are 27% of DR(eye)VE and 18% of MAAD data (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.4). ~3% of DR(eye)VE fixations are in-vehicle (speedometer, mirrors, passengers); 1% are far out of bounds; out-of-view fixations were "erroneously mapped" or removed without marking (`kotseruba2024taskContext` p.3). LBW: not reported whether in-vehicle fixations were excluded (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.4). | **C3.** Eye openness/blink, gaze dynamics and windshield/window/mirror segmentation as suitability metadata. These are suitability indicators, not proof of lower error. |
| L5 | **Aggregation into heatmaps removes the point-level reference, and saliency metrics reward imprecision.** | DR(eye)VE: 1-s window, Gaussian variance 200 px (`palazzi2019dreyeve` p.4). BDD-A: spatial 25 px + temporal 4-frame smoothing (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.5). LBW: one fixation per map with a large kernel; on LBW "the models do not need to be precise to achieve good results" (p.7). DADA: 50 px Gaussian (`fang2022dada` p.4). DriverGaze360: 30-frame window (`govil2026drivergaze360` p.3–4). | **C1.** Point-level reference coordinates and per-sample gaze, separate from gaze-averaged maps (contribution file §C1). |
| L6 | **Raw data are missing, which prevents independent re-analysis.** | LBW: neither raw eye-tracker output nor the eye-camera view is available (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.4). Desideratum 5: "raw data should be made available for re-analysis and reproducibility" (p.8). Re-analysis was possible for DR(eye)VE precisely because it "provides all raw data, including eye tracker output" (`kotseruba2024taskContext` p.2). Per-frame weights and labels used by BDD-A are not available (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.2). DADA was inaccessible to those authors (p.1). Outside driving: "Publishing only the raw data is consequently the minimal requirement to be able to use the data for all use cases" (`jakobi2026livingSurvey` p.11 [V-arXiv]); recommended metadata include "Calibration and validation reports" and "Data quality reporting at all stages" (Fig. 1, p.10). Counter-example: Gong et al. release raw sample-level glasses data (`gong2026eegDataset` p.7). | **C1/C3**, *planned*: release subject to legal/privacy approval. **Do not claim it is released.** |
| L7 | **Vehicle telemetry is minimal, coarse, or missing.** | DR(eye)VE and BDD-A provide GPS, heading, speed, mostly at 1 Hz; no IMU; BDD-A vehicle data is absent for 90 videos; LBW has no vehicle information (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.3). SCOUT needed manual lateral labels because of "noisy and incomplete vehicle information … inaccurate GPS and no yaw output" (`kotseruba2024taskContext` p.7). | **C1.** GNSS/INS, speed, ego-motion. Distinguish installed sensors from modalities actually used for the labels. |
| L8 | **Limited camera field of view and uneven video quality.** | All scene cameras have ≤120° horizontal FOV vs a ~200° human field (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.2). DR(eye)VE's roof camera differs from the driver's view and collects raindrops (p.2–3). >150 BDD-A videos (10%) are off-centre/tilted/obstructed; ~37% have quality issues; ~5% of LBW frames are overexposed (p.2–3). | Mostly **not our focus.** We measure in the wearable scene image; illumination/exposure is recorded as scene metadata (C1/C3). |
| L9 | **Few on-road observers; most datasets are in-lab or simulated.** | On-road: 8 drivers (`palazzi2019dreyeve` p.3), 28 drivers (`kasahara2022lookBothWays` p.7). In-lab passive viewing: BDD-A, MAAD, DADA. In-lab viewers are more centre-biased and miss mirror/side-window checks during yielding (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.3–4). Simulators: LAIA, DriverGaze360, DReyeVR, VR setups (`contreras2026laia`, `govil2026drivergaze360`, `biswas2024gazeSupervision`, `makrigiorgos2019attentionDriving`, `hu2022data`). | **C1.** Real driving in physical traffic. **But we have only 2 participants for the accuracy results**, fewer than any of these datasets. |
| L10 | **Scenario imbalance.** | Maintain/stopped/speed-up = 81% (DR(eye)VE), 78% (LBW), 46% (BDD-A) of frames; lateral actions ≤7%; intersections ~7%, of which 70–80% are right-of-way passes (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations` p.6). | **C1, partial.** Targets deliberately span classes, ego states (114 driving / 38 standstill), static vs moving targets and 7–107 m. **Not** population-representative or balanced. |
| L11 | **Ground truth is not independent of the gaze it labels.** | In all datasets above, the eye-tracker output (after mapping) *is* the label. No independent physical reference is used to check it. Object-of-fixation labels come from annotators who view the gaze overlay (`tawari2018learning` p.3). Jia et al. assign targets by gaze proximity (100 px ≈ 2.5° threshold) (`jia2018gaze` p.3). | **C1.** Announced, verified targets give an *operational* reference. **Caveat (contribution file):** gaze-guided candidate retrieval can make the reference circular; manual review must be shown to guard against this. |
| L12 | **Models trained on gaze assume the labels are correct.** | The authors acknowledge noise: gaze is "a noisy approximation of the causal pixels" (`biswas2024gazeSupervision` p.1); "current datasets of human gaze are noisy and include irrelevant objects" (`liang2023cueing` p.1); gaze-only maps "completely ignore" relevant objects (`pal2020sage` p.2). MAAD tests robustness only to **synthetic** Gaussian noise and affine miscalibration (`gopinath2021maad` p.8–10). No paper here measured the angular error of the gaze used for supervision. | **Motivation for C1/C2.** We do **not** show downstream policy improvement. |

## 5. Precedents we must acknowledge (challenging our own framing)

1. **Traffic objects as calibration targets in a moving car already exist.** `jia2018gaze` corrected wearable-gaze offset with detected stop signs in a moving vehicle (p.1–4). The differences are factual, not a matter of novelty by date: **passenger**, not driver; 3 participants; errors in **pixels**; the correction is evaluated on the same data stream; targets assigned by gaze proximity (p.3–4). Our sparse driving-derived offset benchmark must be framed as an extension (driver, angular error, held-out scenes, structured vs random selection), not as the first use of traffic objects.
2. **DR(eye)VE measured eye-tracker spatial error at 5–15 m** (`palazzi2019dreyeve` p.4). It is not reported and was measured with calibration patterns, not in driving. Still, we cannot say "no driving dataset ever measured tracker error". Say instead that "no measured value is reported" in the dataset paper.
3. **On-road calibration accuracy has been reported** for a *remote* tracker (`stapel2022driverPerception` p.6: typically 1.2°). This is calibration-time, not during driving, and horizontal-only in the analysis.
4. **Manual correction of driving-gaze labels exists**: SCOUT manually re-aligned videos and corrected homography outliers (`kotseruba2024taskContext` p.3). Our manual verification is of **target identity/position**, which is a different object, but reviewers may see it as related.
5. **Raw on-road wearable-gaze release exists** (`gong2026eegDataset` p.7). Our dataset release is **planned only**.
6. **Depth-aware on-road gaze exists**: LBW has 3D gaze with metric stereo depth (`kasahara2022lookBothWays` p.6). Our target range from LiDAR is not unique as a modality; the distinction is target-level range attached to an independent fixation reference.
7. **Announced fixations ≠ spontaneous attention.** None of the datasets above instruct drivers to fixate named targets. This is a limitation of ours for attention modelling, but it is what makes a target reference possible.

## 6. Suggested argument skeleton for the subsection (with keys)

1. **Driving-gaze datasets** come in three forms: on-road wearable (`palazzi2019dreyeve`, `kasahara2022lookBothWays`), in-lab video viewing (`xia2018bdda`, `gopinath2021maad`, `fang2022dada`) and simulation/VR (`contreras2026laia`, `govil2026drivergaze360`, `biswas2024gazeSupervision`). Their gaze is used to supervise or guide driving models (`biswas2024gazeSupervision`, `makrigiorgos2019attentionDriving`, `liu2019gazeModel`, `xia2020periphery`, `abdelkarim2021ggnet`).
2. **Known data limitations:** scenario bias, FOV, telemetry (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations`); processing errors such as missing timestamps and misalignment, blinks/saccades in the labels, and homography outliers (`kotseruba2024taskContext`, `kotseruba2024dataLimitations`); and missing raw data, which prevents re-analysis (`kotseruba2024dataLimitations`, `jakobi2026livingSurvey`).
3. **The gap:** in these papers the eye-tracker output is the label, and its angular error against identified physical targets in real driving is not reported (L1, L11). Measurement was either used internally without a reported value (`palazzi2019dreyeve`), quoted as a spec (`gopinath2021maad`), assumed (`contreras2026laia`), or calibration-time only (`stapel2022driverPerception`). The closest correction precedent (`jia2018gaze`) used passengers and pixel units.
4. **Our contribution**, with its scope limits stated: 2 participants, announced fixations, scene-level splits, release pending.
